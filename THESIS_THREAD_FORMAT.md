# Stock Thesis Thread — The Proven Format

The 5-tweet thesis-thread format that produced the best-performing post on
@TheOneGroupAI ($TE, 40+ likes). Systematized from what actually worked.

## Why this format wins
- **Conviction + a real ticker** — people can act on it, not generic advice
- **Narrative arc** — hook → identity → opportunity → demand signal → honest close
- **Honesty + risk disclosure** — builds trust, doesn't feel like a pump
- **Thread format** — drives dwell time + replies

## The 5-tweet arc (each tweet = 1 reply-chained step)

### Tweet 1 — HOOK (conviction statement, no cashtag needed)
Open with the *idea*, not the ticker. Frame it as a conviction or a bet.
- "Adding a second conviction to my board: ..."
- "I'm building a position around one idea: ..."
- "The market is mispricing X. Here's the thesis."

### Tweet 2 — IDENTITY (what the ticker is)
Name the ticker + company + what it does, in one tight block.
- "$TE — T1 Energy, US solar manufacturing. A former battery maker now building..."
- Include 1-2 concrete numbers (revenue, capacity, contracts) for credibility.
- End with the one-line bet: "The bet: ..."

### Tweet 3 — OPPORTUNITY (why the setup exists)
The structural reason the opportunity is there.
- "Why the US is the opportunity for $TE: ..."
- Crowded globally, scarce domestically. Tariffs/policy pulling supply onshore.
- The edge: what makes THIS company the play.

### Tweet 4 — DEMAND SIGNAL (the quiet tell you watch)
A specific, concrete signal that validates the thesis — not a story.
- "The quiet signal I like on $TE: ..."
- An anchor customer, a contract, a metric. "Real demand, not a story."
- Name the number you watch: "Output is the number I watch: ..."

### Tweet 5 — HONESTY + CLOSE (risk disclosure)
The honest risks + disclaimer. This is what makes it credible.
- "The honest part on $TE: ..." (capital-heavy, policy-dependent, swing factors)
- "I size it as X, not a sure thing."
- "Not financial advice — just the thesis I'm tracking."

## Rules
1. **Every tweet is reply-chained** to the previous (thread, not separate posts).
2. **Concrete numbers** in tweet 2 (revenue, capacity, contracts) — credibility.
3. **A named signal** in tweet 4 — a real tell, not vague optimism.
4. **Honest risk** in tweet 5 — always. No pump energy.
5. **"Not financial advice"** — always close with it.
6. Keep each tweet under ~280 chars (the format above fits).

## Cadence
1-2 thesis threads/week. Each is a new conviction or a follow-up on a tracked name.
