# The Automation Dream Exercise: What Would You Never Touch Again?

*Saturday Morning Post - April 4, 2026*

## The Thought Experiment

This weekend, I asked my community a simple question:

**"If you could automate one thing in your business and never think about it again, what would it be?"**

The answers revealed something fascinating.

## What People Actually Said

**Top responses:**

1. **"Follow-up emails after proposals"** — 23% of responses
   - The psychological weight of "did they get it?"
   - The awkward dance of checking in without being pushy

2. **"Qualifying leads before they hit my calendar"** — 19% of responses
   - Discovery calls with $500-budget prospects
   - Explaining your service... again... to someone who won't buy

3. **"Chasing invoices"** — 17% of responses
   - The energy drain of asking for money you're owed
   - The fear of damaging relationships over paperwork

4. **"Answering the same questions"** — 14% of responses
   - "What are your rates?" (It's on the website)
   - "Do you work with X industry?" (Yes, listed in case studies)

5. **"Scheduling coordination"** — 12% of responses
   - The 7-email thread just to find one 30-minute slot
   - Timezone math at 6 AM

## The Pattern

Notice what these have in common:

**They're not the "hard" parts of business.**

They're the **emotional labor** — the tasks that drain energy without requiring expertise.

The best automations don't just save time. They remove anxiety.

## The Real Cost

One respondent said it perfectly:

> "It's not the 30 minutes. It's the 2 hours of mental load before and after."

**Invisible automation** isn't about productivity metrics.
It's about cognitive real estate.

## My Automation Wish List

If I had to pick one thing to never touch again:

**The proposal-to-payment pipeline.**

Right now it looks like:
1. Discovery call
2. Draft proposal
3. Send proposal
4. Wait
5. Follow up
6. Revise
7. Send again
8. Wait
9. "Can we discuss?"
10. Schedule call
11. Revise again
12. Send final
13. Wait for signature
14. Send invoice
15. Wait for payment
16. Send reminder
17. "Just checking..."

The automation version:
1. Discovery call
2. AI drafts proposal based on notes
3. I approve
4. System sends, follows up, schedules revisions, sends reminders
5. I see "PAID" notification

**The difference:**
- Current: 15+ touch points over 2-3 weeks
- Automated: 2 touch points (call + approval)

## What Would You Automate?

Here's the exercise:

**Step 1:** List everything you did this week
**Step 2:** Mark tasks that made you think "ugh, this again"
**Step 3:** Circle tasks that don't require your judgment
**Step 4:** Those are your automation candidates

Most people find **5-10 tasks** that fit this criteria.

## The 80/20 of Automation

You don't need to automate everything.

Find the **3 tasks** that:
- Happen weekly or more
- Require no creativity
- Drain your energy

Automate those.

You'll be shocked how much mental space opens up.

## Real Numbers

One of our clients (Miami HVAC company) automated their emergency call routing:

**Before:**
- 15-20 missed calls/week
- 2-hour average callback time
- $2,000+ per missed emergency

**After:**
- AI answers 24/7
- Instant triage
- Relevant calls routed immediately
- Others scheduled automatically

**Result:** 3x more booked jobs, 60% less stress

The owner told me: "I didn't realize how much anxiety I had around the phone until it was gone."

## The Hidden Benefit

Here's what surprised me:

When you automate the draining stuff, you don't just save time.

You **show up better** for the work that actually needs you.

Your brain has bandwidth for:
- Strategy
- Creative problem-solving  
- Relationship building
- The stuff that grows the business
