# AI "Picks & Shovels" Opportunities for AI-for-SMB (2026)

**Prepared for:** The One Group (Alec Kennedy) — AI automation agency serving SMBs in South Florida
**Date:** 2026-08-16
**Context trigger:** OpenRouter (AI model gateway/router + billing layer) sold to Stripe for $7B+ — 5x its May 2026 valuation. The founders built a marketplace/aggregator "toll booth" on top of AI models rather than building AI itself.

---

## The Playbook, Decoded

OpenRouter's exit validates a specific thesis: **in an AI gold rush, the durable money is in the layer that sits *between* the model providers and the end users — routing, aggregating, billing, distributing — not in the models themselves.** Stripe paid $7B+ for a "toll booth between AI models" (processing trillions of tokens monthly across 500+ models).

The same logic applies one level down, at the SMB layer. SMBs are the *least* served, *most* numerous, and *least* technically capable buyers in the AI economy. They cannot build, cannot evaluate, cannot integrate, and cannot monitor AI. Every one of those gaps is a "shovel" position.

**The core insight for The One Group:** You are not competing with OpenAI/Anthropic/Stripe. You are competing for the *distribution, aggregation, and trust* layer that sits between those giants and the 33M US SMBs. That layer is fragmented, low-barrier, and — critically — **still winnable by a small, fast, locally-trusted operator.**

---

## Demand Validation (grounding data)

- **Pax8 SMB AI Pulse Report (Q2 2026):** AI adoption among SMBs has "moved" significantly; 402 US SMB leaders surveyed. AI advantage now belongs to small business.
- **Intuit QuickBooks 2026 AI Impact Report:** 34,000+ survey responses across US/CA/UK/AU mapping AI adoption, use, and revenue impact.
- **US Census (May 2026):** AI use at US businesses has grown since December; larger firms (20+ employees) are the biggest users — meaning **sub-20-employee SMBs are the laggards = the untapped market.**
- **SBE Council 2026:** "AI is no longer experimental for small businesses."
- **Vendasta (Jun 2026):** "AI for resellers is no longer a niche play. It is the fastest growing path agencies [use] to turn AI into recurring revenue."

**Bottom line:** Demand is real, growing, and concentrated in exactly the segment (small, non-technical SMBs) that a local agency serves best. The question is not *whether* to play — it's *which* shovel to pick.

## Ranked Opportunities

### #1 — Vertical AI Micro-SaaS for a Specific South Florida Industry

**The shovel:** A purpose-built AI product for ONE vertical (e.g., property management, home services/contractors, medical/dental clinics, real estate, law firms) that wraps models + workflow automation + billing into a turnkey tool SMBs in that niche can just buy.

**Who's the customer:** SMBs in a single vertical (e.g., 50–200 property managers in Miami-Dade/Broward/Palm Beach).

**Monetization:** SaaS subscription ($99–$499/mo) + implementation fee. Recurring, scalable, not hourly.

**Competition:** Vertical AI is the *least* crowded at the SMB level. Harvey (legal) and Cru (construction) prove the model at the top end, but they target mid-market/enterprise. **Almost no one is doing vertical AI micro-SaaS for sub-20-employee SMBs in specific local niches.** The Wishdeal playbook ("Vertical AI in 2026: where the dollars actually are") explicitly calls this the quiet land grab.

**Value vs. difficulty:** **High value / Medium difficulty.** This is the single best risk-adjusted play. It converts your agency's delivery capability into a product with recurring revenue and a defensible niche position. The moat is *domain depth + local trust*, not tech.

**Why unclaimed / timing window:** Horizontal AI is a knife fight against $100B incumbents; vertical SMB niches are too small for VC-backed startups to bother with, but large enough to support a focused operator. **The window is now — before vertical AI SaaS consolidates downward from the enterprise tier.**

**Execution note:** Pick ONE vertical where The One Group already has clients or domain knowledge. Build the first 3–5 deployments as paid pilots with existing clients, productize the repeatable 80%, then sell the packaged version. Do NOT try to be horizontal.

### #2 — White-Label AI Automation "Backend" for Other Agencies

**The shovel:** The One Group becomes the *white-label fulfillment engine* that other agencies (marketing, web, IT/MSP, bookkeeping) resell to their own SMB clients under their own brand. You build/deploy/maintain; they own the client relationship.

**Who's the customer:** Other agencies (marketing agencies, MSPs, web designers, bookkeepers) who have SMB clients asking for AI but no AI capability.

**Monetization:** Wholesale retainer ($500–$2,000/mo per agency partner) + per-deployment fees + revenue share. You're the "OEM" — they're the "brand."

**Competition:** The *platforms* (FlowBots, Vendasta, BotPenguin, younea) are crowded, but they sell *software* to agencies. **Almost nobody offers the *done-for-you white-label service* layer** — the actual building, deploying, and maintaining. That's a services gap, not a software gap, and it's exactly what an agency with delivery capability can fill. Vendasta's own data calls this "the fastest growing path to recurring revenue."

**Value vs. difficulty:** **High value / Low-medium difficulty.** This is a direct extension of what The One Group already does — you're just packaging your delivery as a B2B2C service. No product build required to start; you can begin with a simple partner program and a reseller agreement.

**Why unclaimed / timing window:** Agencies are drowning in AI requests they can't fulfill (E2M, Robofy, MarketWiz all report this). The software platforms exist but leave the *implementation* to the agency — which is exactly the hard part. **The window: agencies are actively seeking fulfillment partners right now; whoever has a proven delivery engine wins the referrals.**

**Execution note:** Start with 2–3 non-competing agency partners (e.g., a web design firm, an MSP, a bookkeeper). Package a standard "AI Starter" (receptionist + lead capture + follow-up) they can resell. White-label everything. This is the fastest path to recurring revenue with zero new product risk.

### #3 — AI Cost & Observability "Control Plane" for SMBs (managed service)

**The shovel:** A managed layer that monitors, tracks, and controls SMB AI spend and agent health — the "Sentry for AI agents" position, but delivered as a *service* for non-technical SMBs rather than a dev SDK.

**Who's the customer:** SMBs that have adopted AI tools/agents and are getting surprise bills, runaway loops, or dead agents — and the agencies (like #2) that need to report cost/ROI to their clients.

**Monetization:** Managed monitoring retainer ($200–$800/mo) + alerting + monthly ROI/cost report. Also a natural upsell to every existing The One Group client.

**Competition:** The *tools* are emerging but fragmented and dev-focused (AspectBase, AgentMetrics, AgentPing, Trakr, KubeNiche — all "one line of code," "SDK," "self-hostable"). **None of them serve the non-technical SMB directly.** The "managed observability for SMBs" position is essentially unclaimed. KubeNiche explicitly frames "LLM token costs [as] the fastest-growing blind spot for SMBs."

**Value vs. difficulty:** **Medium-high value / Low difficulty.** You don't need to build the tool — you can wrap existing open-source tools (AspectBase, AgentMetrics, agent-ledger) in a managed service. The value is in *interpretation and action*, not instrumentation.

**Why unclaimed / timing window:** The observability category is 12–18 months old and still aimed at developers. SMBs are only now hitting the "surprise AI bill" pain point as adoption scales (Pax8, QuickBooks data). **The window: as SMB AI spend grows, the cost-control layer becomes mandatory — and it's not yet claimed at the SMB level.**

**Execution note:** This is a *wedge* — it gets you into every client's AI stack, gives you a defensible recurring revenue line, and positions you as the "AI CFO." Bundle it with #1 and #2.

### #4 — AI Workflow Template / Playbook Library (productized content + services)

**The shovel:** A library of battle-tested, vertical-specific AI workflow templates and playbooks (prompts, agent configs, SOPs, integration recipes) that SMBs and other agencies can buy and deploy.

**Who's the customer:** SMBs (buy the template + done-for-you install) and other agencies (buy the template library to accelerate their own delivery).

**Monetization:** One-time template sales ($49–$499), template library subscription ($99/mo), and — the real money — **the templates as lead magnets that convert into implementation retainers.**

**Competition:** implo.ai and Sigrix sell AI assets/prompts, but they're generic and uncurated. **A *curated, vertical-specific, proven-in-production* template library is differentiated** — most "prompt marketplaces" are junk. The moat is that your templates come from *real client deployments*, not theory.

**Value vs. difficulty:** **Medium value / Low difficulty.** Low risk, fast to launch, and it compounds — every client engagement produces reusable templates. The downside: templates alone are low-margin and easily copied. **Treat them as a marketing asset, not the core business.**

**Why unclaimed / timing window:** The generic prompt-marketplace wave is dying (implo's own tagline admits "AI tools should work for you, not confuse you"). **The window: SMBs are overwhelmed by tool confusion; a trusted, curated, vertical-specific playbook is the antidote — and it feeds every other opportunity here.**

**Execution note:** Package every The One Group deployment into a reusable template. Publish 10–20 free ones as SEO/lead magnets, sell the full library, and use it to shorten sales cycles on #1 and #2.

### #5 — AI Readiness Audit at Scale (productized, not bespoke)

**The shovel:** A fixed-price, standardized AI readiness audit (workflow mapping + stack scoring + top-3 AI opportunities + ROI estimate) delivered in 7–14 days — run as a repeatable product, not a bespoke consulting engagement.

**Who's the customer:** SMBs (and mid-market) that know they "should do AI" but don't know where to start.

**Monetization:** Fixed fee ($1,500–$5,000) per audit + **the audit as the front door to implementation retainers** (the real revenue). Also sellable to other agencies as a white-label lead-gen tool.

**Competition:** **Crowded** — Stack Consulting, Softosmith, Cyberlobe, Eccordia, Ignashev, AIReady, Catalia all offer this. Low barrier to entry; it's commoditizing. **However:** most are solo practitioners with no delivery engine behind them. The One Group's edge is that the audit *feeds a proven implementation pipeline* — most competitors can't actually build what they recommend.

**Value vs. difficulty:** **Medium value / Low difficulty.** The audit alone is a commodity; its value is as a **lead-generation and trust-building mechanism** that converts into higher-margin implementation work. Don't build a business on audits; build a business on what audits unlock.

**Why unclaimed / timing window:** The audit space is crowded but *shallow* — it's a race to the bottom on price with no fulfillment. **The window: whoever pairs the audit with a real delivery engine wins the conversion. The One Group already has the engine.**

**Execution note:** Productize the audit (fixed scope, fixed price, 7–14 days), use it as the top of a funnel that feeds #1, #2, and #3. This is a *conversion tool*, not a standalone business.

### #6 — AI Agent Marketplace for SMBs (local/vertical niche) — *CAUTION*

**The shovel:** A curated marketplace where SMBs browse, buy, and deploy pre-built AI agents for their specific industry.

**Who's the customer:** SMBs (buyers) and AI builders (sellers).

**Monetization:** Marketplace fees (10–30% take rate), listing fees, or SaaS subscription.

**Competition:** **Very crowded** — UpAgents, Moltify, AgentKeep, Cruxis, Sigrix, Agent Arcade, AgentGO, implo. All early-stage, all fighting the marketplace cold-start problem (no buyers without sellers, no sellers without buyers). **This is the hardest position to win** and the most likely to be crushed by a platform (OpenRouter/Stripe, OpenAI, or a big agency platform) moving down.

**Value vs. difficulty:** **Low-medium value / High difficulty.** The toll-booth economics are attractive in theory (that's the OpenRouter playbook), but a small agency cannot win a two-sided marketplace against funded entrants. **This is the "moonshot" the task warned against.**

**Why unclaimed / timing window:** It's not really unclaimed — it's *over-claimed* and under-won. **Recommendation: do NOT build a general marketplace. If you want marketplace economics, do it as a *curated vertical marketplace* inside #1** (e.g., "the AI agent store for South Florida property managers") where you control both sides and the niche is too small for VCs.

### #7 — Local AI Concierge / "AI Done For You" for South Florida SMBs — *CAUTION*

**The shovel:** A local, high-touch service that sets up and maintains AI (receptionist, lead capture, booking, follow-up) for South Florida SMBs — the "AI concierge for SMBs."

**Who's the customer:** Local SMBs (clinics, law firms, real estate, contractors, restaurants).

**Monetization:** Setup fee + monthly retainer ($300–$1,500/mo).

**Competition:** **Already saturated in South Florida** — DonQuijotech (Miami), dArt Studio (Broward/Palm Beach), Sinnux, HolFe, ai50m (Miami), Exordium (Miami), Digismart (Miami), iGreen (South Florida). This is a **red ocean locally.** Pure "we build AI for local businesses" is the most crowded, lowest-moat position available.

**Value vs. difficulty:** **Low-medium value / Low difficulty.** It's easy to start (it's what The One Group already does) but it's a commodity service with thin moats and price competition. **Recommendation: do NOT make this the strategy.** Use it only as the *delivery mechanism* for #1–#5, not as the positioning. The differentiation must come from vertical focus, productization, or the observability/control layer — not from "we do AI for local businesses."

### The recommended strategy (how they stack)

**The One Group should NOT pick one — it should build a funnel that combines the top five:**

1. **Front door:** Productized AI readiness audit (#5) + free vertical template library (#4) as lead magnets.
2. **Core product:** Vertical AI micro-SaaS for ONE chosen South Florida industry (#1) — the defensible, recurring-revenue center.
3. **Scale engine:** White-label fulfillment for other agencies (#2) — turns delivery into B2B2C recurring revenue without new product risk.
4. **Sticky layer:** Managed AI cost/observability control plane (#3) — the "AI CFO" wedge that locks in every client and every agency partner.

**Avoid:** general agent marketplace (#6) and generic local concierge (#7) as standalone strategies — both are crowded, low-moat, and don't leverage The One Group's real advantage (delivery capability + local trust).

### The timing window (why now)

- OpenRouter's $7B+ exit just **re-rated the entire "toll booth" thesis** — capital and attention are flowing to infrastructure/aggregation layers, and SMB-facing versions of these are still unclaimed.
- SMB AI adoption is **accelerating** (Pax8, QuickBooks, Census) but sub-20-employee firms are the laggards — the exact segment a local agency serves.
- Vertical AI is a **"quiet land grab"** (Wishdeal) — horizontal is a knife fight, vertical SMB niches are too small for VCs but big enough for a focused operator.
- The observability/cost-control layer is **12–18 months old and dev-focused** — the SMB managed-service version is open.
- Agencies are **actively seeking white-label fulfillment partners** right now (Vendasta, E2M, Robofy all report the demand).

**The window is open now, and it closes as (a) vertical AI SaaS consolidates downward, (b) observability tools add SMB-friendly managed tiers, and (c) more agencies build their own delivery engines. Move on the vertical + white-label combination first.**
