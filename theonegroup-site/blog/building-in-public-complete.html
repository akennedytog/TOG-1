🚀 Week 1: The Foundation (March 7-14)

Starting point: Idea and a Twitter account

I started with a simple premise: help small businesses actually use AI. Not the AI you read about in headlines—the practical kind that saves hours of repetitive work.

Day 1-2: Infrastructure

Domain, website, Calendly, basic CRM. Speed over perfection. The goal was to have something live I could iterate on.

Day 3-4: The First Conversations

I reached out to 10 SMB owners with one question: "What's one task that eats up your time but doesn't need your specific expertise?"

The answers were remarkably consistent:

  • Follow-up emails after quotes
  • Scheduling and calendar management
  • Basic customer support responses
  • Data entry from forms to spreadsheets

None of them said "I need AI." They said "I need time back."

Day 5-7: First Automation

I built a simple system for an HVAC company: read incoming quote requests, draft personalized follow-ups, queue for human review. Not fancy. But it saved them 2 hours per week.

Week 1 Lesson: SMBs don't care about AI. They care about results. Frame everything in hours saved or revenue gained.

📈 Week 2: First Wins (March 14-21)

Starting point: 1 client, proof of concept

Monday morning, I got on a call with that HVAC company's owner. I didn't pitch AI. I asked about his follow-up process.

"It's a mess," he said. "Leads come in, I mean to call them back, but I'm on a job, then it's 9 PM, and I'm exhausted."

I showed him a 2-minute demo. He signed up on the spot: $500 setup, $200/month. First revenue.

Testing Three Approaches

That week I tested different messaging:

  • "I build AI automation" → Crickets
  • "I help businesses save time" → Mild interest
  • "What task eats your time but doesn't need your expertise?" → Conversations

Thursday I closed a second client—a bookkeeping firm drowning in receipt categorization. Same process: identify pain, show solution.

Week 2 Lesson: Lead with the problem, not the technology. Pricing is about value, not hours. Both clients would have paid more—they're saving 10+ hours/month.

🧵 Week 3: Systems & Scale (March 21)

Starting point: 2 clients, $400 MRR

This week changed everything.

The Realization: Automation vs Scheduled Posting

Most AI "automation" is just scheduled posting. Real automation learns from engagement and adapts.

We built a system that tracks which hooks get replies vs. likes. The data surprised us: specificity beats hype. "Saved 10 hours/week" outperforms "Revolutionize your workflow" 3:1.

The Feedback Loop

The real moat isn't the AI—it's the feedback loop. Every post teaches the next one. Every interaction refines the approach.

We built a dashboard showing:

  • Content types that drive replies (high intent)
  • Content types that drive likes (reach)
  • Hooks that convert to website visits
  • Calls-to-action that actually work

The Autonomous Decision

Thursday, something unexpected happened. Our AI analyzed engagement data and suggested we test a new hook format. It noticed questions outperformed statements 2.5x.

I approved the test. Results: 47% higher engagement.

This is the future: AI suggests, humans decide.

3 More Clients

We hit our Week 3 goal: 5 total clients across three industries:

  • HVAC company: Follow-up automation
  • Bookkeeping firm: Receipt categorization
  • Real estate agent: Lead qualification
  • Marketing agency: Content repurposing
  • Consultant: Proposal generation
Week 3 Lesson: The businesses winning with AI aren't the ones with the biggest models. They're the ones with the tightest feedback loops. Speed of iteration beats size of model. Every time.

🎯 The Big Lessons

After 21 days, here's what actually matters:

1. Concrete beats abstract

"Saved 10 hours/week" > "Revolutionize your workflow"

2. Problems first, solutions second

Nobody wakes up wanting AI. They wake up wanting their evenings back.

3. Speed of iteration beats perfection

We shipped something imperfect in Week 1 and improved it based on real feedback. Waiting for "ready" means never launching.

4. AI is a multiplier, not a replacement

The best results come from human + machine, not machine alone.

5. Building in public creates accountability

When you say things publicly, you follow through. Plus, you build a network of people on similar journeys.
